How one question becomes cited evidence
The selected hybrid path: both searches use the same resolved question and eligible chunks. Numbers come from this report’s saved configuration and structure-aware build.
Exact local search
Cosine similarity as linear algebra
Rows of C are unit-normalized chunk vectors; q is the unit-normalized question vector. One matrix multiplication gives one cosine score per eligible chunk.
There is no chunk-to-chunk similarity matrix. Cosine scores are geometric similarities, not answer probabilities.
Why two searches?
BM25 rewards matching words, rare terms and useful frequency while adjusting for passage length. It helps with exact park and trail names.
Embeddings match related meanings even when wording differs. RRF combines the two ranked lists rather than adding incompatible raw scores.

Table follow-ups can prioritize named rows from the candidate pool before context packing; the original retrieval ranking remains in the trace.
